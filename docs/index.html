Mismatch Market

Are defenses putting linebackers on the wrong tight ends and running backs? NFL 2021, Weeks 1-8, man-coverage dropbacks. Code and write-up.

A linebacker on a tight end costs the defense 0.4 yards of separation. Offenses can buy that matchup by lining the tight end up inline, and defenses are not steering linebackers away from the players who win it.

1. The market: hover any player for his scouting card

Left = defenses give him more linebackers than normal for where he lines up. Up = he gets more space than normal when they do. The shaded corner is an offensive coordinator's target list and a defensive coordinator's to-fix list. Bars are one standard error: one player's Punishment over eight weeks is noisy, so read the corner as candidates, not a ranking.

2. Scout a defense: where does it put linebackers?

Pick a defense to see how often it covers tight ends and running backs with a linebacker from each alignment, against the league average. A red bar well above grey is a matchup that defense hands out more than most. n = routes faced, so small bars are thin evidence.

3. What it looks like: press play

One completed pass from the shaded corner, with the matched defender highlighted and the separation updating every tenth of a second. Drag the slider to step through it.

Matchups are inferred from player tracking (each route runner is paired with one coverage defender by smallest average distance from snap to throw), not charted. Data: NFL Next Gen Stats tracking and PFF scouting from the NFL Big Data Bowl 2023 release.
